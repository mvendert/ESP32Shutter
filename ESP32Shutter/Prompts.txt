We are going to be implementing a ESP32 shupper release control unit using 
an ESP32 Wroom32 devboard for a canon R5 camera. For that we are using the remote 
shutter jack (TRS Jack) with an 4N36 optocoupler.
The Canon R6 features a 2.5 mm stereo (TRS) ‘Remote’ jack (Canon’s “E3” remote terminal) 
for wired shutter release (compatible with Canon’s RS-60E3 remote switches)   . 
This jack has three contacts – typically: Sleeve = Ground, Ring = Focus (half-press), 
Tip = Shutter (full-press). The camera’s design is such that shorting specific 
contacts to ground triggers the camera: connecting the focus contact to ground 
initiates autofocus, while connecting the shutter contact to ground releases the shutter 
(takes a photo).
Because the camera’s remote circuit carries a small voltage (~3.2–3.3 V and ~0.5 mA) 
on the focus/shutter lines, it’s important to avoid directly wiring the ESP32’s 
GPIO pins into the camera. Direct connection could create ground loops or risk damage 
to the camera’s electronics if something goes wrong.
We have some 4N36 optocouplers, some 2.5mm trs plug cables, and resistors of a 
multitude of resistors at hand. We also have some pushbuttons and a rotary variable 
resistor as often used in Arduino demo projects at hand.
We tend to treat the optocoupler as a bridge between the ESP32 and the camera, 
like a push-button that closes the camera’s shutter circuit when the ESP32 outputs a 
HIGH signal. Here’s how to wire it for one of two connections. So repeat for forcus and 
shutter triggering.
•	Optocoupler Input (LED) Side: Connect an ESP32 digital output pin to the 
optocoupler’s anode through the current-limiting resistor. Connect the optocoupler’s 
cathode to the ESP32 ground (GND). When the ESP32 sets this 
pin HIGH, current flows through the LED, illuminating it inside the optocoupler. 
(Be sure to also connect the ESP32 ground to the negative side of your power source 
and the ground of the opto’s input.)
•	Optocoupler Output (Transistor) Side: The optocoupler’s output is a 
phototransistor that behaves like a switch when the internal LED is lit. 
Connect the phototransistor’s collector to the camera’s shutter release wire 
(the 2.5 mm plug’s shutter contact), 
and connect the phototransistor’s emitter to the camera’s 
ground (the 2.5 mm plug’s sleeve). When the optocoupler LED is activated by the ESP32, 
the phototransistor conducts, effectively shorting the camera’s shutter line to ground, 
which triggers the shutter release   . 
(If you also need autofocus control, use a second optocoupler similarly to short the 
focus contact to ground when commanded. 
Each optocoupler effectively replaces one of the two switches in a traditional remote 
shutter.
In practice, you would replicate the output side for two channels if you need 
separate focus and shutter control (each optocoupler acting like a switch for
